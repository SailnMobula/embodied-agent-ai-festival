## The robot does not know where its hand is

No sense of touch, no eye watching itself. All it knows is how far each motor has turned.

Turning that set of angles into "the hand is _here_" is **forward kinematics** — pure geometry, no
AI involved. The tip of the chain has a name: the **end effector**, the hand that touches the world.

## Try it

<ForwardKinematicsDemo client:visible />

- Every joint drags everything downstream of it along.
- One degree at the shoulder moves the hand further than one degree at the wrist. That is why the
  joints closest to the body carry the expensive motors.

The other direction — _"put the hand there, work out the angles yourself"_ — is **inverse
kinematics**: often many answers, sometimes none. Not today.

## Where the angles come from

> **Live demo — the robot waves.** Play the taught wave and watch the joint angles scroll past.
> Run it twice: the same motion, to the degree. Then look at what is _not_ in that file — no idea
> where the person is, no answer if someone steps into the arm's path.

Teaching wins whenever the motion _is_ the goal: a gesture, a fixed pick-and-place, anything that
has to look the same in front of an audience. Its limit is hard — a recording cannot react.

## Standing is not a recording

Balance is a reaction. The floor is uneven, the load shifts, someone nudges the robot in the back.
No list of angles answers a shove, so walking is not taught. It is trained.

1. **Simulate.** Thousands of copies of the robot walk in parallel, far faster than real time.
2. **Reward.** Points for staying upright and moving forward; penalties for falling, flailing,
   burning current. Nobody describes a gait — the gait is whatever scores well.
3. **Randomise, then transfer.** Vary mass, friction, sensor delay, terrain. A policy that survives
   that spread has a chance on real hardware. What it does not survive is the **sim-to-real** gap.

The result is a habit, not a trajectory: sensors in, joint targets out, fifty times a second,
forever. Ask why it moved that one ankle and there is nothing to read. Which is also why balance
never gets handed to a language model — it runs far below it.

## Both, on the same robot

- **Wave** — taught. Exact, repeatable, blind.
- **Stand, balance, walk** — trained. Reactive, robust, unexplainable.

In Station 5 the agent calls both as skills and cannot tell which is which. It decides _when_,
never _how_.
