## The robot does not know where its hand is

A robot arm has no sense of touch and no eye watching itself. All it knows is how far each motor
has turned. Turning that set of angles into "the hand is *here*" is called **forward kinematics**,
and it is pure geometry — no AI involved.

The tip of the arm has a name: the <strong>end effector</strong> — the business end, the hand or
gripper that actually touches the world.

## Try it

Move the sliders. Each one turns one joint. Watch the position readout change.

<ForwardKinematicsDemo client:visible />

Two things worth noticing:

- Every joint moves everything downstream of it. The shoulder drags the elbow, the wrist and the
  hand along with it.
- A small change at the shoulder moves the hand much further than the same change at the wrist.
  This is why the joints closest to the body need the most precise motors.

## The harder direction

Going the other way — "put the hand *there*, figure out the angles yourself" — is **inverse
kinematics**, and it is genuinely difficult: there are often many valid answers, and sometimes
none. The X2 solves this constantly while walking. We are not opening that box today.
