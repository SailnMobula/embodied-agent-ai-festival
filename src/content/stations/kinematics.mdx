## Same description, you and the robot

<JointsAndLinks />

- **Joint**: a place that turns
- **Link**: the rigid piece between two joints, your bones
- **Angle**: how far a joint is turned

## Angles in, position out

<ForwardKinematicsDemo client:visible />

- Pure geometry, no AI: **forward kinematics**
- The last point is the **end effector**, the hand
- Every joint drags everything after it
- A degree at the shoulder moves the hand further than a degree at the wrist

## Teaching is recording

Press **Record**, move the sliders, press **Replay**.

- Angles plus timestamps, nothing else
- Replay is exact, every time
- It looks at nothing and reacts to nothing

> **Live demo: the robot waves.** A file of joint angles, played back on real hardware. Run it
> twice and it is identical to the degree.

## Learning from demonstrations

<ImitationLearning />

- Cameras, depth and joint positions, recorded in step
- A hundred runs of the same task, done by a person in VR
- No reward, no rules, only examples
- Same idea as the recorder above, except it generalises to boxes it never saw

The model in the middle is a **VLA**: vision, language and action in one network. We trained one on
our own lifting data.

## Learning from reward

<LocomotionTraining />

- No data, no demonstrations
- A reward says what counts: upright, forward, smooth, no crash
- Randomised mass, friction, delay and terrain, so the policy survives outside the simulator

Nobody writes a gait. The gait is whatever scores best.

Out comes a **policy**: sensors in, joint targets out, fifty times a second. Ask why it moved that
ankle and there is no answer to read.
